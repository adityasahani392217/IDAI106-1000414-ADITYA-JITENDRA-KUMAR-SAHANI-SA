# Problem Statement & User Research Insights
## CareEase Hospital Queue App | Design Thinking — Define Stage

---

## Research Synthesis

From 12 surveys, 5 detailed empathy maps, and 10 personas, the following critical insights were identified:

---

## Top 10 Actionable Insights

| # | Pain Point | Design Insight | Priority |
|---|-----------|----------------|----------|
| 1 | No queue position updates | App must show real-time position and estimated wait time, updated every few minutes | Critical |
| 2 | Missed name calls (deaf/hard of hearing) | Silent vibration + visual notification when turn is within 5 minutes | Critical |
| 3 | Nothing to do while waiting | Distraction Hub with music, VR, videos, breathing exercises | High |
| 4 | Children have no stimulation | Dedicated Kids Zone within Distraction Hub with age-appropriate content | High |
| 5 | Medical anxiety unaddressed | Mood check-in feature + calming empathy messages based on stress level | High |
| 6 | Language barriers | Full Hindi language support + multilingual interface option | High |
| 7 | Elderly cannot use complex apps | Large text mode, minimal steps, accessibility-first design | High |
| 8 | Inaccurate time estimates | Transparent system: show a range, not a single number; update dynamically | Medium |
| 9 | No guidance for first-time visitors | "What to Expect" walkthrough on first app launch | Medium |
| 10 | Caregiver not informed | Link patient token to a companion's phone for shared notifications | Medium |

## Problem Statement (Primary)

> **"How might we transform the hospital waiting experience into a calm, engaging, and informative journey — for patients of all ages, abilities, and languages — by providing real-time queue transparency, inclusive accessibility features, and meaningful emotional support?"**

---

## Problem Statements by User Group

### For the Anxious Patient (Jitendra, Ramesh)
> "How might we give patients clear, reliable queue information so that uncertainty — the primary driver of stress — is eliminated from the hospital waiting experience?"

### For the Elderly Patient (Priya, Lalita)
> "How might we design an accessible, low-complexity notification system that ensures elderly and hearing-impaired patients never miss their turn and always feel safe and informed?"

### For the Young Tech User (Aaron, Arhaan, Karan)
> "How might we create a hospital waiting experience that meets the digital expectations of young adults — with real-time tracking, calming content, and app-based alerts?"

### For the Parent with Child (Sneha)
> "How might we reduce the stress of parents accompanying sick children by providing accurate wait times and engaging, age-appropriate distraction content for children?"

### For the Caregiver Companion (Ishant, Gudiya)
> "How might we support unpaid caregivers by giving them the same real-time information and alerts as the patient — acknowledging their essential and exhausting role?"

## Validated Design Principles for CareEase App

Based on all research, the CareEase app must be:

1. **Transparent** — patients must always know where they stand in the queue
2. **Calm** — visual design, language, and interactions must reduce anxiety, not add to it
3. **Inclusive** — accessibility features are default, not optional
4. **Multilingual** — Hindi and English at minimum
5. **Simple** — elderly and low-tech users must be able to use core features with minimal steps
6. **Engaging** — meaningful content that passes time without increasing screen fatigue
7. **Proactive** — the app alerts the patient; the patient does not have to hunt for information

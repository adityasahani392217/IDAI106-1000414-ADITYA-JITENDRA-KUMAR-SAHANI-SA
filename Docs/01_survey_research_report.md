# User Research — Survey Report
## CareEase Health Systems | Hospital Queue Experience Study
**Conducted by:** Design Thinking Research Team  
**Scenario:** Smart Solutions for Hospital Queues  
**Total Respondents:** 12  
**Location:** Mumbai, Maharashtra  
**Method:** In-person interviews + short written survey at hospital waiting areas  
**Date:** March 2025

---

## Research Objective

To understand the real emotional, physical, and informational pain points experienced by patients and their companions while waiting in hospital queues — and to identify what would genuinely improve their experience.

## Key Findings

### Average Wait Times
- Minimum: 35 minutes (Sudiksha Mourya)  
- Maximum: 130 minutes (Lalita Devi)  
- **Average: 81 minutes**

### Average Stress Level
- Scale 1–5: **3.6 / 5** (High)  
- 5 out of 12 respondents rated stress 4 or above

### Information Gap
- **10 out of 12** respondents received no updates about their wait time
- Most had to approach staff multiple times — and found staff unhelpful or too busy

### Coping Mechanisms (Top Responses)
1. Scrolling phone (9/12)
2. Listening to music (5/12)
3. Walking around (4/12)
4. Trying to distract a child or companion (2/12)
5. Reading (2/12)
6. Sitting quietly and worrying (2/12)

### App Helpfulness Rating
- Average: **4.5 / 5**
- 11 out of 12 said an app would definitely or likely help

### Accessibility Concerns
- 2 respondents have hearing difficulties (Priya Mehta, Lalita Devi)
- 1 respondent has mild vision difficulty (Priya Mehta)
- 1 respondent (Lalita Devi) does not own a smartphone — needs kiosk/screen alternatives

### Preferred Distractions
| Preference | Count |
|------------|-------|
| Calming music / mood-based playlist | 7 |
| Short videos or entertainment | 5 |
| Breathing exercises / mindfulness | 4 |
| Kids interactive zone | 2 |
| VR/AR nature environment | 3 |
| Audio relaxation | 3 |
| Large display screen updates | 2 |

## Key Pain Points Identified

1. **No real-time queue updates** — patients have no idea how long they will wait
2. **Poor communication from staff** — asking for updates is awkward and often unhelpful
3. **No engagement or distraction** — patients are left with nothing to do
4. **Physical discomfort** — hard chairs, back pain after long waits
5. **Anxiety about medical outcomes** — especially for older patients
6. **Missing turn due to loud announcements** — critical for hearing-impaired patients (Lalita Devi missed her name twice)
7. **No child-friendly zone** — parents struggle to manage young children
8. **Language barrier** — Hindi-speaking patients find English-only displays confusing
9. **Lack of accessibility features** — no visual alerts, no large-text options
10. **Companion fatigue** — caregivers like Gudiya Sahani and Ishant Vishwakarma have no support either

## Conclusion

The research confirms that the hospital waiting experience is a significant source of stress, particularly due to the complete absence of queue information, poor physical comfort, and a total lack of engagement or distraction. Vulnerable groups — elderly patients, children, and hearing-impaired individuals — face disproportionately higher distress. An inclusive, multi-modal app that provides real-time updates, calming distractions, and accessibility features would meaningfully transform this experience.

# User Personas — CareEase Hospital Queue App
## Design Thinking | Empathize Stage
**Total Personas: 10**  
**Scenario: Smart Solutions for Hospital Queues**

---

## Persona 1 — Jitendra Sahani

| Field | Details |
|-------|---------|
| **Name** | Jitendra Sahani |
| **Age** | 40 |
| **Gender** | Male |
| **Occupation** | Factory Floor Supervisor |
| **Location** | Dharavi, Mumbai |
| **Visit Reason** | Monthly diabetes check-up, outpatient department |
| **Tech Comfort** | Medium — uses WhatsApp and news apps daily |
| **Language** | Hindi (primary), basic English |

**Background:**  
Jitendra works 10-hour shifts at a textile factory and manages a team of 30 workers. He is the sole earner in his family of four. His diabetes was diagnosed 2 years ago and he must visit the hospital monthly. Every visit means taking half a day off work — unpaid. The longer the wait, the more anxious and frustrated he becomes.

**Goals:**
- Know exactly how long the wait will be so he can plan around work
- Get in and out as quickly as possible
- Receive diabetes-related tips or updates while waiting
- Not miss his turn

**Pain Points:**
- Gets no information about his queue position
- Has to ask staff repeatedly, which feels embarrassing
- Worried about missing work and losing wages
- Hard plastic chairs cause back discomfort after 30 minutes

**Quote:**  
*"I came at 10am. It is now 12pm. No one tells me anything. I feel like I'm invisible here."*

**Behaviour Pattern:** Checks phone constantly, walks around every 15 minutes out of restlessness, watches the door anxiously.

## Persona 2 — Gudiya Sahani

| Field | Details |
|-------|---------|
| **Name** | Gudiya Sahani |
| **Age** | 39 |
| **Gender** | Female |
| **Occupation** | Homemaker |
| **Location** | Dharavi, Mumbai |
| **Visit Reason** | Accompanying husband Jitendra |
| **Tech Comfort** | Low-Medium — uses WhatsApp, video calls with family |
| **Language** | Hindi only |

**Background:**  
Gudiya comes with her husband whenever he has a hospital appointment. She manages the household and their two children. She is used to waiting but finds long hospital visits tiring. She sits quietly, prays, and tries not to bother anyone. She has no one to talk to and often feels overlooked.

**Goals:**
- Understand what is happening with her husband's treatment
- Find something to pass the time comfortably
- Not feel out of place or confused in an English-heavy environment

**Pain Points:**
- Everything is in English — she cannot read signs
- No Hindi interface anywhere
- Chairs are uncomfortable for long waits
- Feels helpless and worried but tries not to show it

**Quote:**  
*"I just sit and wait. I don't know where to look or what to ask. I wish someone would just tell us what is happening."*

**Behaviour Pattern:** Sits with hands in lap, makes eye contact with other waiting patients, occasionally whispers with her husband.

## Persona 4 — Aaron D'Souza

| Field | Details |
|-------|---------|
| **Name** | Aaron D'Souza |
| **Age** | 21 |
| **Gender** | Male |
| **Occupation** | Second-year Engineering Student |
| **Location** | Bandra, Mumbai |
| **Visit Reason** | First-ever solo hospital visit — general health checkup |
| **Tech Comfort** | Very High — smartphone-native, uses apps for everything |
| **Language** | English (primary), basic Hindi |

**Background:**  
Aaron has never visited a hospital alone before. He came for a routine checkup his college made mandatory. He is not sick, just nervous about what the procedure involves. He expected the process to be smoother — like booking a cab or ordering food. Instead, he found a chaotic waiting room with no digital guidance.

**Goals:**
- Understand each step of the process clearly
- Get a real-time estimate of how long he will wait
- Receive a notification on his phone when his turn is near

**Pain Points:**
- No digital queue system — feels very outdated
- No one explained what happens after he registered
- Cannot tell if he is in the right area or the right queue

**Quote:**  
*"I book food, cabs, movies — all on an app. Why can't I at least track my turn in a hospital?"*

**Behaviour Pattern:** On phone, reads articles, checks the time every 5 minutes, occasionally asks a nurse for updates.

## Persona 6 — Ishant Vishwakarma

| Field | Details |
|-------|---------|
| **Name** | Ishant Vishwakarma |
| **Age** | 18 |
| **Gender** | Male |
| **Occupation** | Student |
| **Location** | Govandi, Mumbai |
| **Visit Reason** | Accompanying grandmother (ENT department) |
| **Tech Comfort** | High |
| **Language** | Hindi and Marathi |

**Background:**  
Ishant brought his grandmother to her ENT appointment since she cannot manage alone. He is now responsible for both of them — making sure she does not miss her turn, that she is comfortable, and that she understands what is happening. The hospital gave no guidance. His grandmother's name was called twice on a PA system she could not hear, and they nearly missed the appointment.

**Goals:**
- Receive a silent notification or visual alert when his grandmother's turn approaches
- Find an activity that is suitable for both him and his grandmother
- Feel that the hospital is caring for them

**Pain Points:**
- PA announcements are useless for hearing-impaired patients
- No accessibility features anywhere in the hospital
- He feels overwhelmed managing an elderly person alone

**Quote:**  
*"They called her name twice on the speaker. She couldn't hear. We nearly lost the slot. This is not okay."*

**Behaviour Pattern:** Alert and attentive, frequently checks the counter, keeps a hand on grandmother's shoulder, visibly stressed.

## Persona 8 — Priya Mehta

| Field | Details |
|-------|---------|
| **Name** | Priya Mehta |
| **Age** | 65 |
| **Gender** | Female |
| **Occupation** | Retired School Teacher |
| **Location** | Borivali, Mumbai |
| **Visit Reason** | Cardiology follow-up |
| **Tech Comfort** | Low — has a smartphone but struggles with small text and complex apps |
| **Language** | Hindi (primary), some English |
| **Accessibility Needs** | Mild hearing difficulty, mild vision difficulty |

**Background:**  
Priya is a retired teacher who lives with her daughter. She has a heart condition and visits the cardiologist every 6 weeks. She is extremely anxious during these visits. Her daughter drops her off but cannot stay. The waiting room is overwhelming — she cannot read small signs, cannot follow English announcements, and does not know how to use the hospital's app. She waited 110 minutes in a state of quiet terror.

**Goals:**
- Be notified of her turn through her phone (large text, vibration)
- Feel reassured that she has not been forgotten
- Access information in Hindi with large, readable text

**Pain Points:**
- Cannot read small font on hospital displays
- Very anxious about her heart — the wait makes it worse
- No one checks on her
- Would not know how to use a complex app alone

**Quote:**  
*"I just sat and prayed. I was so afraid. What if they forget me? What if my heart does something?"*

**Behaviour Pattern:** Sits very still, clutches her bag, frequently looks toward the counter, takes deep breaths.

## Persona 9 — Ramesh Gupta

| Field | Details |
|-------|---------|
| **Name** | Ramesh Gupta |
| **Age** | 58 |
| **Gender** | Male |
| **Occupation** | Senior Accountant |
| **Location** | Andheri, Mumbai |
| **Visit Reason** | Orthopedic consultation — knee replacement assessment |
| **Tech Comfort** | Medium — uses email, WhatsApp, banking apps |
| **Language** | Hindi and English |

**Background:**  
Ramesh has chronic knee pain and has been referred for a possible surgery assessment. He is anxious about the diagnosis and physically uncomfortable sitting on hard hospital chairs for extended periods. He arrived early but ended up waiting 95 minutes. He asked staff three times and received unhelpful responses each time.

**Goals:**
- Understand his expected wait time
- Find a comfortable sitting position (or be told if there are chairs with armrests)
- Calm his nerves about the potential surgery

**Pain Points:**
- Hard chairs worsen his knee pain
- No one informs him of delays
- Anxiety about surgery recommendation is not addressed in any way
- Feels disrespected when staff are dismissive

**Quote:**  
*"I am in pain sitting here. My knee hurts. And nobody even acknowledges that I exist."*

**Behaviour Pattern:** Shifts position frequently, grimaces, reads news on phone, asks staff questions.

## Persona 10 — Sneha Patil

| Field | Details |
|-------|---------|
| **Name** | Sneha Patil |
| **Age** | 32 |
| **Gender** | Female |
| **Occupation** | Software Engineer |
| **Location** | Powai, Mumbai |
| **Visit Reason** | Pediatrician visit — 4-year-old son Arjun has a fever |
| **Tech Comfort** | Very High — uses apps, Figma, Google Workspace daily |
| **Language** | English and Marathi |

**Background:**  
Sneha is a working mother who took the morning off work to bring her son Arjun to the doctor. Arjun is feverish, fussy, and crying on and off. The waiting room has nothing for children — no toys, no screens, no dedicated area. Sneha has tried giving him YouTube but the hospital Wi-Fi is bad. She is also working remotely on her laptop, trying to manage both her son and a deadline.

**Goals:**
- Accurate wait time so she can plan her work calls
- A kids' entertainment section in the app to distract Arjun
- Notification on her phone so she can step away briefly if needed

**Pain Points:**
- App showed 30-minute wait — it was actually 70 minutes
- Her son is miserable with nothing to do
- She cannot focus on work and manage the child simultaneously
- Feels judged by other patients when Arjun cries

**Quote:**  
*"If there was just something for my son to look at — even a cartoon — it would change everything."*

**Behaviour Pattern:** Alternates between laptop and managing child, looks exhausted, frequently checks phone, bribes child with snacks.
